The Last Territory

How highways and development reshaped the survival of the Florida panther.

Once found throughout the southeastern United States, the Florida panther’s range extended across millions of acres of forest and wetland. By the mid-20th century, hunting and rapid urban expansion reduced the population to fewer than 30 individuals.

Conservation efforts helped increase population numbers, but road mortality remains one of the leading documented causes of death. The data below shows how mortality and recovery have changed over time.

Population Recovery

1970s — Near Extinction

Estimated 20–30 panthers remained.

1995 — Genetic Rescue

Texas cougars were introduced to increase genetic diversity.

2000s — Recovery Begins

Population estimates began steadily rising.

Today — Fragile Stability

Modern estimates range between 120–230 panthers.

Source: FWC Florida Panther Population Estimates.
